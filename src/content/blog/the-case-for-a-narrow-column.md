---
title: The case for a narrow column
date: 2026-08-12
description: Why constraining line length is one of the highest-leverage typography decisions you can make.
tags: ['Typography', 'Layout']
---

Most portfolio sites are too wide. Not visually — structurally. The reading measure runs the full width of the viewport, and every paragraph becomes a chore to track. Here's why I keep my content column deliberately narrow.

## Line length is a readability lever

The classic guidance is 45–75 characters per line, and it holds up. Past that, your eye has to travel too far to find the start of the next line, and you lose your place. Below it, the rhythm breaks up too often.

A fixed, narrow column takes that variable off the table entirely. The measure is right at every viewport size because it never changes.

### It also shapes hierarchy

When the column is narrow, you don't need heavy type or loud color to create hierarchy. The whitespace on either side does the work. A single medium-weight heading against a wall of space reads as important without shouting.

## Constraints make decisions cheaper

Every time the canvas is wide open, you have to decide how wide *this* thing should be. A fixed measure removes that decision. Cards, images, and text all snap to the same rail, and the page feels composed instead of assembled.

- One column to reason about
- One set of horizontal paddings
- One place where responsive behavior lives

## What about images?

Full-bleed imagery still works — you just let it break out of the measure intentionally, as an exception. The exception reads as deliberate precisely *because* the rest of the page holds the line.

Narrow isn't a limitation. It's the frame that makes everything inside it feel considered.
